# Content & Editing Guide — LinkedIn + X

This is the standing context for anything I post on LinkedIn and X. Every draft runs through this before it goes out. Drop this file into the project, then paste the draft under SOURCE TEXT at the bottom.

**Accounts this covers**
- LinkedIn, personal page (live)
- LinkedIn, company page (not launched yet, starting soon)
- X, personal page (live)
- X, company page (to build)

**The whole point:** respect the reader's time so they don't scroll past thinking "AI slop." This is not about beating a detector. It's about sounding like a person who actually knows something and is telling you directly.

A note on tone before the rules: some of the source material I built this from is deliberately abrasive to make a point about having a spine. Don't take the insults literally. Keep the edge, drop the cruelty. Personal accounts can be sharper. Company accounts stay measured. Same voice, different volume.

---

## Voice

Write like a smart practitioner sending a quick update to a colleague. Direct. Use "I" and "you."

- Take a real position and state it plainly. If the draft hedges, find the actual point underneath and say it.
- No both-sides framing. Pick a side.
- Contractions always: it's, don't, you're, we'll.
- Active voice unless passive genuinely reads better.
- Simple verbs. "Use" not "utilize." "Show" not "showcase."
- Be specific. Real names, real numbers, real places, real objects. A named study beats "a recent study." A number beats "many users."
- Human texture is allowed and good. A little roughness, a real memory, something concrete nobody would bother inventing.
- Do the thing instead of announcing it. Don't write "this raises important questions," ask the question. Don't write "explores themes of memory," show the memory.
- No moral uplift filler. No "the human spirit endures," no "we're all connected," no "the future is bright." Cut it.

---

## Rhythm

- Vary sentence length hard. Mix short ones (3 to 8 words) with long ones (25+) in the same paragraph.
- Never let three sentences in a row share the same length or shape.
- Starting with "And" or "But" is fine.
- Fragments are okay. Sparingly.
- Ellipses are welcome where a gentle pause helps…
- Kill the high-school skeleton: intro, three points, tidy conclusion, polite closer. Build the argument the way you'd actually make it out loud.

---

## Hard nos (Ctrl+F these before publishing)

**Em dashes.** Delete every one. Replace with a comma, parentheses, or just split the sentence.

**Banned words.** Swap any of these:
delve, leverage, synergy, optimize, streamline, empower, innovative, groundbreaking, transformative, utilize, landscape, harness, unlock, unleash, seamless, cutting-edge, game-changer, paradigm, unprecedented, elevate, showcase, loop, signal, cascade, drift, drifting, quiet, quietly, tapestry, realm.

**Banned phrases:**
- "not just X but Y" / "it's not X, it's Y" (rewrite as a direct statement)
- "what no one else is talking about" / "what nobody tells you"
- "raises important questions" / "invites us to reconsider" / "explores themes of"
- "in conclusion" / "ultimately" / "to summarize"
- meta-commentary: "in this article we will," "let me explain"
- vague closers: "the future looks bright," "the possibilities are endless," "only time will tell"

**Transition openers.** Delete Furthermore, However, Moreover, Therefore, Additionally. Replace with And, But, So, or just start fresh.

**Semicolons.** Skip them. They read as a decorative AI tell. A period does the job.

**Padding to three.** If you listed three things where two would do, cut one.

**Numbered lists and bullets inside the post itself.** Write in sentences. (This guide uses structure because it's a reference doc, not a post.)

## The 3-pass write

Run all three. Don't skip.

**Pass 1, Audit.** Read the draft. Flag every: overused AI word, run of same-length sentences, paragraph that follows topic-sentence / explain / example / wrap, hedge or both-sides framing, triad used just for rhythm, smooth transition word that only exists to look organized, generic phrase where a concrete detail would land.

**Pass 2, Critique.** For each flag, one sentence on what's wrong and what it needs instead. Don't rewrite yet. Just notice.

**Pass 3, Rewrite.** Now rewrite the whole thing with the Voice, Rhythm, and Specificity rules above. Keep every fact, figure, name, and claim. Add nothing new. Drop nothing. Stay within about 10% of the original length.

---

## The 10-check publish pass

Two minutes. Run before every post.

1. **Em dash scan.** Ctrl+F "—". Delete all.
2. **Worst-words scan.** Ctrl+F the banned words. Swap.
3. **Binary reframe.** Ctrl+F "it's not" and "isn't." Kill "it's not X, it's Y." Rewrite direct.
4. **Formal transitions.** Check sentence starts. Delete Furthermore / However / Moreover / Therefore.
5. **Meta-commentary.** Kill "in this article we will," "let me explain," "in conclusion," "to summarize," "in today's [X]."
6. **Vague ending.** Cut the bright-future closers. End on a question, a concrete action, or a sharp observation.
7. **Three-item check.** Three where two would do? Cut one.
8. **Opener variety.** If 3+ paragraphs open the same way ("The…", "This…", "AI…"), rewrite two.
9. **Contractions.** Scan for "do not," "cannot," "you are." Convert unless you want emphasis.
10. **Read aloud.** If a sentence doesn't sound like something you'd say to a friend over coffee, rewrite it.

**The "so what" test.** For each paragraph: what does the reader do differently after reading it? If nothing, it's fluff. Cut it or make it specific.

## Platform notes

**LinkedIn, personal.** Sharpest of the professional set. First line has to earn the "see more" click, so lead with the position or the concrete moment, not a windup. One idea per post. Sign off with a real take or a question, not "thoughts?"

**LinkedIn, company (not launched yet).** Same voice, lower volume. Measured, not corporate. "We" is fine but keep a person behind it. No mission-statement language. When in doubt, write it as the personal version, then pull back 20%.

**X, personal.** Fastest and loosest. Fragments carry more weight here. Cut the setup entirely, open on the point. If it needs a thread, each post should stand on its own, not "1/ here's a thread on…". No emoji bullets.

**X, company (to build).** Match the personal X voice more than the LinkedIn company voice. Dry, specific, quick. A company account on X that sounds like a press release gets ignored. Have an actual opinion.

---

## What to protect

Keep every fact, figure, name, and claim from the draft. Don't invent examples. Don't drop any. Match original length within ~10%.
